Project overview

Project title
Cottage dock replace
Project purpose
We plan to remove and replace a failing wooden dock at a private cottage on the north shore of the lake. The existing dock was built in 1978 on timber cribs filled with fieldstone. The decking has rotted through in several places, two of the cribs have shifted out of line, and stone from the cribs has spilled onto the lake bed. The structure is no longer safe to walk on and we have kept it closed since last summer. The new dock will be a floating dock, roughly 12 metres long and 2 metres wide, held in place by four helical anchors driven into the lake bed. Floating docks sit on the surface of the water, so the new structure will not sit on the bottom and will not need any fill. We chose this design after speaking with a local contractor and with the lake association, who told us that several neighbours have moved to floating docks for the same reason. All work will happen from the shore and from a small barge. No machinery will enter the water. We will remove the old timber cribs by hand and by winch and haul the fieldstone above the high water mark, where it will be reused for shoreline stabilization behind the dock. The rotted timber will be trucked to the municipal transfer station. We will install a silt curtain around the work area before we begin and leave it in place until the water runs clear. Work is planned for late August and early September, outside the spring spawning window identified by the provincial fisheries office. We expect the work to take about ten days. After the dock is installed we will replant the disturbed section of bank with native shrubs and grasses supplied by a regional nursery, and we will keep the replanted area fenced until it takes hold. We are not proposing any dredging, blasting or change to the shape of the shoreline. The footprint of the new dock is smaller than the structure it replaces, and removing the cribs should return about 18 square metres of lake bed to open substrate.
